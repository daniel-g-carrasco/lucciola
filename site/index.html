lucciola

A rock-solid, ready-to-use PC operating system built on Fedora Atomic.

All the benefits of an atomic system, the speed-tuned CachyOS kernel, and the endless scrolling desktop of niri and Noctalia.

install

your system

Lucciola builds on Fedora Silverblue and adds a desktop made of niri and Noctalia, plus a set of everyday and creative apps.

GNOME ships too, as a backup desktop: you can choose it on the login screen if niri and Noctalia ever give you trouble.

your workspace

niri places windows in columns on a strip wider than the screen. Move along it with the keyboard, mouse or touchpad gestures. Windows never overlap, and new ones never shrink the others.

Noctalia gives the bar, app launcher, notifications, control center and lock screen a consistent look. Both come from Fedora’s official software collection.

updates

The whole system ships as one package, called an image. Updates are fully automatic: they download in the background and apply at your next restart, leaving nothing half-updated. If one goes wrong, choose the previous version in the boot menu.

Thanks to its atomic heart, the base of the system is read-only; your files, settings and apps live apart from it.

day one

Lucciola starts with apps such as Zen Browser, Thunderbird, LibreOffice, GIMP, Inkscape, darktable and OBS Studio already installed. Bazaar, the app store, lets you easily install more from Flathub’s collection.

Audio and video codecs such as H.264, H.265 (HEVC) and AAC come preinstalled, so common audio and video formats play right away.

For developers: Distrobox and Toolbox run tools in separate environments without changing the system, and one command adds VS Code and Homebrew.

foundations

  1. Fedora Silverblue
  2. Universal Blue
  3. Lucciola

Lucciola is Secure Boot friendly: it signs the CachyOS kernel, the system’s core, with its own key. Once you register that key, Secure Boot keeps checking signatures at startup. Your PC’s TPM security chip can unlock the encrypted disk at boot. Your device verifies every update’s signature before installing it.

for you

Lucciola is for people who want a safe, modern, creator-friendly and keyboard-friendly desktop without assembling it themselves. You need x86-64-v3 support, a set of features in most 64-bit x86 CPUs made since about 2015.

I’m Daniel, the only maintainer of this experimental open-source project. Write to me if you run into problems or have suggestions.

before you start

Lucciola is experimental and made by one person, so keep a copy of your files before you switch.

It needs a processor with x86-64-v3 support. To check yours, run:

/lib64/ld-linux-x86-64.so.2 --help | grep x86-64-v3

If the command prints a line with “supported”, you are ready. If it prints nothing, Lucciola’s usual kernel will not start on this PC: take the version with Fedora’s kernel instead (see fedora’s kernel).

AMD and Intel graphics work out of the box. Lucciola does not yet ship NVIDIA’s own driver.

You switch to Lucciola from a Fedora Atomic system, such as Silverblue, Kinoite, or Bluefin. Lucciola has no installer image yet, so on an empty disk you install Fedora Silverblue first.

switch to lucciola

  1. Turn Secure Boot off, if it is on

    Lucciola signs its kernel with its own key. Your PC does not know that key yet, and with Secure Boot on it refuses to start the kernel. mokutil --sb-state tells you whether Secure Boot is on. To turn it off, restart into the firmware settings with this command; you will turn it back on in step 5.

    systemctl reboot --firmware-setup

    If your disk unlocks on its own through the TPM, keep the passphrase at hand: after this change, the PC asks for it once.

  2. Switch

    This command downloads Lucciola next to your current system, which stays as it is until you restart.

    sudo rpm-ostree rebase ostree-unverified-registry:ghcr.io/daniel-g-carrasco/lucciola:stable
    systemctl reboot

    The first :stable comes out with Fedora 45, planned for 27 October 2026; until it exists, the command stops with “manifest unknown”. To try Lucciola before then, write lucciola:candidate, the test build, and run the same command with :stable once it is out.

  3. Log in to Lucciola

    On the login screen, choose your name, click the gear button in the bottom-right corner, and pick Lucciola. The login screen remembers your choice.

    During the first minutes, your apps install in the background; GIMP, Inkscape, darktable, and OBS Studio arrive last. Lucciola also switches itself to signature-checked updates, and that change applies at the next restart.

  4. Register Lucciola’s key

    Restart once more. Before Lucciola starts, a blue screen appears: press a key within 10 seconds, choose Enroll MOK, Continue, and Yes, type the password margine, and choose Reboot. That screen uses a US keyboard layout.

    If you miss it, the screen comes back at the next restart. This command makes it wait for you:

    ujust lucciola-secureboot enroll

  5. Turn Secure Boot back on

    This command tells you whether the key is in place and what to do next:

    ujust lucciola-secureboot

    Then restart into the firmware settings with systemctl reboot --firmware-setup and turn Secure Boot on. Leave the factory keys as they are.

    If you prefer to keep Secure Boot off, ujust lucciola-secureboot never stops the reminders.

With a mouse, hold Super and Shift and turn the wheel to move along the strip. On a touchpad, swipe with three fingers.

The settings (Super ,) hold the wallpaper, the bar, the dock, and the colors. Your own niri settings, such as the touchpad speed, go in ~/.config/niri/lucciola.kdl. Keep Awake, in the bar, stops the PC from sleeping, now or until a time you choose.

apps

Bazaar, the app store, installs apps from Flathub. They update on their own twice a day, except when the battery is low or the connection is metered.

A preinstalled app you remove stays removed: Lucciola remembers it and does not install it again. An app that leaves Lucciola’s default set stays on your PC. ujust lucciola-slim removes some of the larger apps in one go: it lists what it removes and asks before doing anything. ujust lucciola-slim-restore brings back the default apps you removed.

For developer tools, use a Toolbox: a Fedora container with its own dnf, where you install anything without touching the system.

toolbox create
toolbox enter

Distrobox does the same with other distributions, such as Ubuntu or Arch.

One command adds the developer tools: VS Code, a C compiler, and Homebrew for command-line tools. It lists what it installs and asks first. Restart when it is done.

ujust lucciola-devmode

Add status to see what is in place, or remove to take it away.

updates

Lucciola checks for updates every day. It downloads them in the background and prepares the new version next to the running one; a notification tells you when it is ready. The new version starts at your next restart, and Lucciola never restarts on its own.

To update now, run:

ujust lucciola-update
systemctl reboot

ujust lucciola-status shows which version you run and whether a newer one is waiting.

going back

Every update keeps the previous version. If something goes wrong after an update, restart, tap ↓ while the boot menu shows, and choose the second entry. To stay on that version, run:

sudo rpm-ostree rollback
systemctl reboot

To leave Lucciola, switch back to the system you had. For Fedora Silverblue, run:

sudo rpm-ostree rebase ostree-unverified-registry:quay.io/fedora/fedora-silverblue:44

Your files, settings, and Flatpak apps stay. If your disk unlocks through the TPM, the PC asks for the passphrase once.

fedora’s kernel

Lucciola runs the CachyOS kernel. A second version, the same in everything else, runs Fedora’s own kernel: it starts on processors without x86-64-v3, and it is the way out if a CachyOS kernel update misbehaves on your PC. It is rebuilt with every update of the usual version.

To switch to it, run this command and restart:

ujust lucciola-kernel fedora
systemctl reboot

ujust lucciola-kernel shows which kernel you run, and ujust lucciola-kernel cachyos brings the CachyOS kernel back. Your files, settings, and apps stay either way.

If your disk unlocks through the TPM, the first start after a switch asks for the passphrase. Once it has started, run ujust lucciola-tpm-unlock enable and the TPM unlocks it again.

On a processor without x86-64-v3, switch to Lucciola straight onto Fedora’s kernel: in step 2 of switch to lucciola, write lucciola:fedora-kernel in place of lucciola:stable. Fedora signs its own kernel, so it starts with Secure Boot on, and you can skip step 1.

This version is published together with the first :stable, planned for 27 October 2026. Until then there is none to switch to, and ujust lucciola-kernel fedora changes nothing.

disk unlock

If your disk is encrypted, the TPM security chip can unlock it at startup, and you type only your login password. This needs Secure Boot on and Lucciola’s key registered.

ujust lucciola-tpm-unlock enable

Then lock the boot menu, so that changing how the system starts takes a password. You choose it, at least eight letters and digits; the user name is root. Normal starts need no password, and neither does going back to a previous version.

ujust lucciola-grub-lock on

Replace enable or on with status to see where you are, or with disable or off to undo. The disk passphrase keeps working throughout.

help

This command checks the system and lists anything out of place:

ujust lucciola-doctor

To report a problem, run the command below. It writes a summary of your system to lucciola-report.txt in your home folder and opens the page for a new issue, where you attach the file.

ujust lucciola-report

Things to know:

  • niri has no color management yet. Lucciola loads your screens’ calibration curves instead, so the night light cannot change a calibrated screen.
  • If the screen turns red, the lock screen has stopped. Super Ctrl L brings it back.
